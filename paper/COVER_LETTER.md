# Draft cover letter

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Same Words, Different Procedure:
Counterfactual Transfer Tests for Reusable LLM-Agent Skills,” as an original
research article.

The paper addresses a practical measurement problem in reusable-agent systems:
success after supplying a skill does not establish that the skill caused the
success, and semantic similarity can hide procedural incompatibility. We
introduce CSTT, a six-condition same-state protocol that crosses source
procedural compatibility with surface similarity and includes no-skill and fair
binding-only controls. Every source is backed by a successful native ALFWorld
training trajectory, while target gold plans and source entities are excluded
from runtime skill inputs.

Following two retained failed pilots and a final mechanism revision, we froze
the complete method, source assignments, three local model revisions,
statistics, decision gates, and a 60-target zero-overlap confirmation roster.
Across 180 target-model blocks, a procedurally matched but lexically far source
achieved 97.8% native success, compared with 10.0% for a lexically near
procedural lure. The paired gain was 87.8 percentage points (95% CI
82.8--93.3; task-clustered randomization p = 1.00 x 10^-6). It remained
positive in all three model families and all six task families. Relative to the
binding-only control, matched skills produced 150 helpful and zero harmful
discordances. Three independent audits accepted all 1,080 episodes and 46,662
native turns without a state, action, source-assignment, or information-boundary
failure.

The package includes English and Chinese manuscripts, complete trajectories,
frozen hashes, tests, environment records, and a reproducibility archive. It is
not under consideration elsewhere. Authors will complete identity, funding,
conflict, contribution, data-license, and journal-specific declarations before
submission.

Sincerely,

Anonymous Authors
